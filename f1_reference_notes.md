## History & Evolution — Summary

Formula One (F1) is the highest class of worldwide racing for open-wheel, single-seater formula racing cars, run by the Formula One Group and sanctioned by the **Federation Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA)**. The FIA Formula One World Championship has been one of the world's premier forms of motorsport since its **inaugural running in 1950** and is widely considered to be the pinnacle of motorsport.

The word *formula* in the name refers to the set of rules all participant cars must follow. A Formula One season consists of a series of races known as **Grands Prix**, which take place in multiple countries and continents on either purpose-built circuits or closed public roads (street circuits).

A **points scoring system** is used at each Grand Prix to determine two annual World Championships:
- **Drivers' World Championship**
- **Constructors' World Championship** (synonymous with teams)

Each driver must hold a valid **Super Licence** — the highest class of racing licence issued by the FIA — and all races must be held on **Grade One tracks**, the FIA's highest circuit rating.

Formula One cars are the **world's fastest regulated road-course racing cars**, achieving extraordinary cornering speeds through massive aerodynamic downforce generated by front and rear wings and underbody tunnels. The cars are complex machines dependent on electronics, aerodynamics, suspension, and tyres.

## Major Rule Changes & Regulatory Eras

### 1994 — The Ban on Electronic Aids
- Traction control, launch control, and automatic shifting were **first banned**.
- Marked a shift toward emphasizing **driver skill** over technological intervention.
- Made the cars more challenging and unpredictable to drive.

### 2001–2008 — The Oscillating Ban
- Electronic aids were **briefly reintroduced in 2001**.
- Banned again in **2004**, then **fully banned in 2008**.
- Reflected the ongoing tension between F1's technological innovation mandate and its roots in raw driving talent.

### Cost Cap Era (2021–present)
- Introduced to **level the playing field** between wealthy and smaller teams.
- As of the **2026 season**, the cap stands at **US$215 million** (plus a separate **$130M Power Unit cap**).
- **Exclusions** (e.g., certain senior salaries, marketing costs) mean actual spending often exceeds the cap.
- Has fuelled significant **financial and political battles** within the paddock.

### 2022 Ground-Effect Cars
- A major aerodynamic overhaul introduced **ground-effect floors** alongside traditional wings.
- Designed to improve **following and overtaking** by reducing turbulent air.

## Structural & Regulatory Themes

- **Dual championship structure**: Drivers' + Constructors' championships create a dual competitive focus.
- **Super Licence requirements**: Serve as a barrier to entry, ensuring only top-tier drivers compete.
- **Grade One circuit standards**: Ensure the highest safety and facility levels at all F1 venues.
- **Evolving cost cap framework**: Continues to shape team budgets and financial politics within the sport.

## Geographic & Commercial Expansion

### UK Engineering Hub
- The **United Kingdom** hosts **6 out of 11** F1 teams.
- Long regarded as the technical and engineering heart of the sport.

### Calendar Expansion Under Liberty Media
- Five new Grands Prix added since 2017: **Miami, Las Vegas, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Netherlands**.
- Reflects a strategic shift toward **global market growth**, particularly in the U.S. and Middle East.
- Growth of **street circuits** (Las Vegas, Miami, Jeddah, Lusail) as spectacle-driven events.
