## History and Evolution — Focused Summary

Formula One (F1) is the highest class of worldwide racing for open-wheel, single-seater formula racing cars, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The FIA Formula One World Championship has been one of the world's premier forms of motorsport since its inaugural running in **1950** and is widely considered the pinnacle of motorsport.

F1 originated from the **World Manufacturers' Championship (1925–1927)** and the **European Drivers' Championship (1931–1939)**. The "formula" refers to the set of rules all participant cars must follow. Before World War II, several Grand Prix racing organisations suggested a new championship to replace the European Championship, but racing was suspended during the conflict. The International Formula was formalised in **1946**, effective in **1947**. The first Grand Prix under the new regulations was the **1946 Turin Grand Prix**. The new World Championship was instituted to commence in **1950**.

The first championship race was the **1950 British Grand Prix** at Silverstone on **13 May 1950**. **Giuseppe Farina** (Alfa Romeo) won the first Drivers' World Championship, narrowly defeating his teammate **Juan Manuel Fangio**. Fangio went on to win the championship in **1951, 1954, 1955, 1956, and 1957** — a record for most World Championships by a single driver that stood for **46 years** until **Michael Schumacher** won his sixth in **2003**.

A **Constructors' Championship** was added for the **1958 season**. **Stirling Moss**, often regarded as one of the greatest F1 drivers of the 1950s and 1960s, never won the Formula One championship — finishing second four times and third three times between 1955 and 1961. Fangio won **24 of the 52 races** he entered, still the record for highest winning percentage by an individual driver.

Non-championship Formula One races were held for many years but declined due to increasing costs. The **last non-championship F1 race** was the **1983 Race of Champions** at Brands Hatch, won by **Keke Rosberg**.

## Key Facts: Major Rule Changes, Landmark Eras & Historical Milestones

### Technological Milestones

1. **Mid-engined cars (1959–1961):** Bugatti introduced mid-engined cars; Jack Brabham (champion 1959, 1960, 1966) proved their superiority. By 1961, all teams had switched. The Ferguson P99 (four-wheel drive) was the last front-engined F1 car in a championship race (1961 British GP).

2. **Monocoque chassis (1962):** Lotus introduced a car with an aluminium-sheet monocoque chassis instead of space-frame design — called the greatest technological breakthrough since mid-engined cars.

3. **Sponsorship introduced (1968):** Team Gunston became the first to run cigarette sponsorship (Gunston cigarettes) at the 1968 South African GP. Lotus followed at the 1968 Spanish GP with Gold Leaf livery.

4. **Aerodynamic downforce & wings (1968):** Colin Chapman installed modest front wings and a rear spoiler on the Lotus 49B at the 1968 Monaco GP. Aerofoils slowly gained importance throughout the season.

5. **Ground-effect aerodynamics (late 1970s):** Lotus introduced ground-effect aerodynamics (previously used on Jim Hall's Chaparral 2J in 1970), providing enormous downforce and greatly increased cornering speeds. Aerodynamic forces pressed cars to the track at up to **5 times the car's weight**.

6. **Turbocharged engines (1977–1989):** Renault pioneered turbocharged engines in 1977. By 1986, a BMW turbo achieved ~**1,300 bhp** in qualifying. Race trim power reached ~**1,100 bhp**. Turbos were banned after 1989.

7. **Electronic driver aids (1980s–2008):** Lotus developed active suspension (first appeared 1983 on Lotus 92; perfected by 1987). Semi-automatic gearboxes and traction control followed. Traction control, launch control, and other electronic aids were **banned in 1994**, briefly **reintroduced in 2001**, then **banned again in 2004 and 2008**.

8. **Hybrid era (2014–present):** 2.4L naturally aspirated V8s replaced by **1.6L turbocharged hybrid power units**. Honda returned as a manufacturer in 2015. Current power units run on E10 fuel (10% bio-component); a 100% sustainable fuel is planned for 2025/2026.

9. **Engine regulations for 2026+:** Internal combustion output increases to 450kW; hybrid-to-engine power split shifts from 53/47 (2026) to 58/42 (2027) to 60/40 (2028).

### Rule Changes & Regulatory Milestones

10. **Concorde Agreements:** The **1981 Concorde Agreement** guaranteed technical stability (teams given reasonable notice of new regulations). Second in **1992**, third in **1997**. A new Concorde Agreement was signed in **August 2020**, committing all 10 teams to the sport until **2025**.

11. **Ground-effect ban (1983):** FISA banned ground-effect aerodynamics from 1983.

12. **Fuel tank & boost limits (1984, 1988):** FIA limited fuel tank capacity in 1984 and boost pressures in 1988 to reduce speeds.

13. **Team orders banned (2002):** After incidents of openly manipulated race results (most famously Ferrari at the 2002 Austrian GP), team orders were banned.

14. **Senna's death & safety reforms (1994):** Ayrton Senna died at the **1994 San Marino Grand Prix** (Tamburello curve). Roland Ratzenberger also died in qualifying that weekend. The FIA then used safety as grounds to impose rule changes that previously required unanimous team agreement, leading to the **"narrow track" era from 1998** — smaller rear tyres, narrower track, grooved tyres to reduce mechanical grip.

15. **Narrow track era (1998–2008):** Cars had smaller rear tyres, narrower track, and grooved tyres. Slick tyres were **reintroduced in 2009**.

16. **Cost cap introduced (2021):** Initial cap of **$175 million**, reduced to **$145 million** (COVID), then **$140 million (2022)**, settling at **$135 million** for three years. For **2026+**, restructured to **$215 million operational cap** + **$130 million Power Unit Cost Cap**.

17. **2022 aerodynamic overhaul:** Major rule and car design change to promote closer racing: **ground effects** reintroduced, new aerodynamics, larger wheels with low-profile tyres, redesigned nose and wing regulations, ban on barge boards. Aimed at reducing "dirty air" turbulence.

18. **COVID-19 token system (2020–2021):** A token system limited which parts could be modified; 2021 aero regulations were pushed back to 2022, with teams using 2020 chassis for two seasons.

### Landmark Eras

19. **Fangio era (1950s):** Juan Manuel Fangio won **5 World Championships** (1951, 1954–57); record stood for 46 years.

20. **Brabham/Lotus innovation era (1959–1970s):** Mid-engined revolution, monocoque chassis, sponsorship introduction, aerodynamic wings, ground-effect development.

21. **Ecclington commercial era (1970s–2017):** Bernie Ecclestone transformed F1 into a multi-billion-dollar business. Bought Brabham in 1971, became FOCA president in 1978. FISA–FOCA war led to Concorde Agreements. Liberty Media acquired commercial rights in **2017 for ~$8 billion**.

22. **Senna–Prost rivalry (1988–1993):** Central focus of F1; ended with Prost's retirement. Senna's death in 1994 was a watershed moment for safety.

23. **Schumacher–Ferrari era (1996–2006):** Michael Schumacher won **5 consecutive Drivers' Championships** and **6 consecutive Constructors' Championships** with Ferrari. Set records for Grand Prix wins, wins in a season, and most championships (7, shared with Lewis Hamilton).

24. **Mercedes–Hamilton dominance (2014–2020):** Hybrid era breakthrough. Mercedes won **16 of 19 races in 2014**, 16 in 2015, record **19 in 2016**. Lewis Hamilton won titles in 2015, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020.

25. **Red Bull–Verstappen era (2021–present):** Honda-powered Red Bull challenged Mercedes. **Max Verstappen** beat Hamilton for the 2021 title after a season-long battle. Won 2022 and 2023 titles. Verstappen became youngest-ever race winner at age 18 (2016 Spain).

### Historical Milestones

26. **First World Championship race:** 1950 British Grand Prix, Silverstone.

27. **First Constructors' Championship:** 1958 season.

28. **First night race:** 2008 Singapore Grand Prix (followed by day-night Abu Dhabi 2009, Bahrain converted to night 2014).

29. **Longest streak without a driver death:** 20 years between Senna (1994) and **Jules Bianchi** (2014 Japanese GP, died July 2015).

30. **Virtual Safety Car introduced (2015):** Implemented at the 2015 Monaco GP after Bianchi's accident, as recommended by the FIA accident panel.

31. **Highest straight-line speed record:** **378 km/h (234.9 mph)** by Valtteri Bottas, qualifying for 2016 European GP. In-race record: **372.6 km/h (231.5 mph)** by Juan Pablo Montoya, 2005 Italian GP.

32. **Calendar expansion:** Inaugural 1950 season had **7 races**. **2024 season had 24 races** — the highest number ever. New races added under Liberty Media: Miami, Las Vegas, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Dutch.

33. **Points system (current, since 2010):** Top 10 cars score points; winner gets 25 points. Driver must complete 90% of race distance to receive points.

34. **Tyre suppliers:** From 1998–2008, grooved tyres (no slicks). Slicks reintroduced 2009. Bridgestone became sole supplier 2007–2010 after Michelin withdrawal (2005 US GP incident). Pirelli is the current exclusive supplier.

35. **F1 Academy (2022):** All-female single-seater championship created to "change perceptions and inspire the next generation of girls." Inaugural champion: Marta García (Prema Racing).
