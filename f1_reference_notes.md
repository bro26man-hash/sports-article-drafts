# Formula One — Reference Notes

## History & Evolution — Focused Summary

Formula One (F1) is the highest class of worldwide racing for open-wheel, single-seater formula racing cars, run by the Formula One Group and sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The FIA Formula One World Championship has been one of the world's premier forms of motorsport since its inaugural running in **1950** and is often considered the pinnacle of motorsport.

The word "formula" in the name refers to the set of rules all participant cars must follow. A Formula One season consists of a series of races known as Grands Prix, held in multiple countries and continents on either purpose-built circuits or closed road courses (street circuits). A points-scoring system determines two annual World Championships: one for drivers and one for constructors (teams).

The commercial landscape of F1 has evolved dramatically. The commercial rights were held by British business magnate **Bernie Ecclestone** for decades before being acquired by **Liberty Media Corporation** in **2017** for an estimated **$8 billion**. Under new ownership, five new Grands Prix were added to the calendar: Miami, Las Vegas, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the Netherlands.

## Key Facts: Major Rule Changes, Landmark Eras & Historical Milestones

### Foundational Rules & Structure
- **1950**: The first FIA Formula One World Championship season marks the birth of modern F1 racing.
- The "formula" in F1 denotes a strict set of rules governing car specifications, which all participants must follow.
- Each driver must hold a valid **Super Licence** — the highest class of racing licence the FIA issues.
- Races must be held on **Grade One** tracks, the highest circuit rating the FIA awards.

### Origins & Founding
- Formula One originated from two predecessor championships:
  - **World Manufacturers' Championship (1925–1927)**
  - **European Drivers' Championship (1931–1939)**
- The FIA formally established the Formula One regulations in **1946**, effective from **1947**.
- The first Grand Prix under the new regulations was the **1946 Turin Grand Prix**.
- The inaugural FIA Formula One World Championship season commenced in **1950**.
- The first world championship race was the **1950 British Grand Prix** at **Silverstone Circuit** on **13 May 1950**.
- **Giuseppe Farina** won the first Drivers' World Championship (Alfa Romeo).
- **Juan Manuel Fangio** won championships in **1951, 1954, 1955, 1956, and 1957** — a record 5 titles that stood for **46 years** until Michael Schumacher won his 6th in 2003.
- Fangio won **24 of 52 races entered** — still the record for highest F1 winning percentage.

### The Constructors' Championship & Early Milestones
- **1958**: The Constructors' Championship was added to the World Championship.
- **1962**: Lotus introduced a car with an **aluminium-sheet monocoque chassis** — the greatest technological breakthrough since the mid-engine revolution.
- **1968**: **Sponsorship was introduced** to the sport. Team Gunston was first with cigarette sponsorship; Lotus followed with Gold Leaf livery at the Spanish Grand Prix. **Aerofoils (wings)** also appeared, introduced by Colin Chapman at Monaco.
- **1983**: The last non-championship Formula One race — the Race of Champions at Brands Hatch, won by Keke Rosberg.

### Major Rule Changes
- **1994**: Traction control, launch control, automatic shifting, and other electronic driving aids were **first banned** — a landmark regulatory shift emphasizing driver skill.
- **2001**: Electronic driving aids were **briefly reintroduced**.
- **2004 & 2008**: Electronic aids were **banned once more**, reinforcing the philosophy that driver talent, not technology, should determine outcomes.
- **2014**: Start of the **Hybrid Era** — 1.6L V6 turbo hybrid power units with energy recovery systems (ERS), a massive technical and regulatory overhaul.
- **Cost cap (2026 season)**: Standings at **US$215 million**, though actual team costs often exceed this due to exclusions — a major contemporary financial regulation.

### Commercial & Expansion Milestones
- **1970s**: **Bernie Ecclestone** rearranged the management of F1's commercial rights, transforming the series into a multi-billion dollar business. He bought Brabham in 1971, gained a FOCA seat, and became FOCA president in 1978.
- **1979–1981**: The **FIA–FOCA war** — a power struggle over television revenues and technical regulations, resulting in the **1981 Concorde Agreement** guaranteeing technical stability. Additional Concorde Agreements followed in **1992** and **1997**.
- **2017 — Liberty Media acquisition**: New ownership ushered in a fresh strategic direction, adding five new Grands Prix (Miami, Las Vegas, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Netherlands) and expanding the calendar's global reach.
- The new era also brought increased media coverage of F1's financial and political battles, reflecting the sport's growing mainstream prominence.

### Technical Evolution
- Modern F1 cars depend on advanced **electronics, aerodynamics, suspension, and tyres**.
- **Aerodynamic downforce**: F1 cars are the world's fastest regulated road-course racing cars, generating enormous downforce through front and rear wings and underbody tunnels.
- The ongoing tension between cost control, technical innovation, and competitive parity defines the sport's regulatory landscape.
